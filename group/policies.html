Laboratory Safety

  • ALL group members must complete the online “EHS Laboratory Safety Training — Standard Chemical” program through MyPath. Go to mypath.rochester.edu, click Enter MyPath, and log in with your NetID.
  • Training must be renewed yearly. Submit proof of completion to Prof. Porosoff by July 31st each year.
  • Official laboratory hours are M–F, 9 am–5 pm. Graduate students and undergraduates working over the summer are expected to be in the lab / lab office during this period, barring extenuating circumstances with explicit permission. Schedules have some flexibility for appointments or illness/personal reasons — use your best judgment; you generally do not need to give explicit notice unless it conflicts with a scheduled group event or you will be away for an extended time.
  • Graduate students may work in the lab alone once trained. Undergraduates may work alone once trained and given explicit permission.
Buddy system If undergraduates work in the lab outside normal hours (nights / weekends), they MUST use the buddy system. The only exception is briefly entering the lab to shut down an experiment.

Data Management

All collected data must be backed up to the shared cloud folder under a folder with your name on Box. Details of all experiments must be recorded in the group electronic laboratory notebook on LabArchives. Ensure electronic data files can be referenced to the appropriate section of the electronic lab notebook. Contact Prof. Porosoff if you need access to Box or LabArchives.

05

Manuscript / Abstract / Poster Deadlines

All documents must be submitted no later than two weeks prior to the conference or abstract-submission deadline. The only exception is if a group meeting is scheduled as a practice talk for a conference.

Approval required All submitted abstracts, posters, etc. require full approval prior to submission — these documents reflect on the entire group, and we need enough time to iterate toward a high-quality product.
06

Laboratory Responsibilities

Waste management

Monitor laboratory waste levels and file the online pickup form through Campus Optics. Waste pickup requests should be submitted every two weeks to once a month depending on level. Check satellite waste-container levels and request orders for new waste containers.

Safety

Monitor potential safety hazards in the lab: ensure all experiments/syntheses are properly labeled, ensure compatible chemical storage, and check for trip hazards and other concerns. Flush eyewash stations and complete the log weekly.

Labeling requirement ALL experiments, beakers, and syntheses MUST carry a label with, at minimum: (1) contents, (2) date, (3) initials of the responsible party.

General housekeeping

Maintain workstations, bench coverings, waste containers, balances, etc. Keep work areas clean and tidy and return all tools/parts to their storage area after use. Do not leave dirty glassware in the sink — responsible parties must do their dishes, and glassware on the drying rack should be returned to storage once dry.

Ordering

Group orders are collected and submitted to the order channel on Box regularly. Urgent orders with prior consent can be sent directly to Prof. Porosoff. Preferred vendors: Thermo Fisher (Alfa Aesar), Sigma-Aldrich, McMaster-Carr, VWR, Electron Microscopy Sciences, Micromeritics, Harrick, Omega, Cole-Parmer, Grainger. Check on deliveries regularly.

Gas cylinder management

Cylinder levels must be checked before the start of every experiment, and orders placed when a cylinder is low (~500 psig). The gas manager updates the gas-cylinder inventory sheet on a biweekly (M/F) basis to avoid interruptions: gas cylinder inventory sheet.

Chemical inventory

The first person to arrive each day checks GVT 123 for packages and barcodes, and tags all new chemicals. Update the inventory spreadsheet with CAS numbers, chemical name, container size, and barcode; log chemicals when containers are empty.

Log before use Log new chemical arrivals BEFORE use to facilitate yearly reconciliation. Keep the inventory up to date for ALL new arrivals: chemical inventory sheet.

Miscellaneous lab tasks

Acid/base bath monitoring and replacement; Drykeep monitoring and desiccant regeneration.

Equipment booking

Be mindful of fellow group members when booking equipment. If an experiment is <12 h, book the equipment only for the necessary number of hours to ensure efficient use. Equipment cannot be booked for more than three consecutive days.

Work / Vacation / Absence Policies

Adapted from the URMC trainee handbook.

  • Remote desktop software is permitted to facilitate data analysis and running experiments. This is a privilege that may be revoked at any time without notice.
  • Graduate students are expected to be motivated and driven in their experimental work. Running out of a gas or liquid nitrogen is not an excuse to stop experiments — know in advance when a consumable is low and order a replacement. If we do run out of LN2, borrow from other labs (URNano, etc.). Short work-stoppages add up and can significantly delay projects, publications, and graduation.
  • Graduate students may take two weeks of vacation (10 business days) each calendar year (Jan 1–Dec 31). Vacation does not automatically accrue and can be rolled over only with written permission of the advisor.
  • Email Prof. Porosoff well in advance (3–4 weeks notice) for any planned vacation. Winter-holiday time off is not a problem — just submit dates with plenty of notice.
  • Semester breaks are not automatic holidays. Graduate students are expected to be in their labs or offices during breaks and periods when no classes are held.
  • International students traveling outside the U.S. must submit a Short Term Absence Notice so the school can assist in the event of extended security clearance before re-entry.
  • All students are entitled to time off for official University holidays: New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving plus the following Friday, and Christmas Day.

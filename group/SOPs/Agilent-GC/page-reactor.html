Reactor Setup & Running a Reaction

Preparing and loading the catalyst, reduction and pressurization, and running the GC sequence from bypass through to a reaction — the day-to-day operation that produces the data you'll analyze.

01

Prepare the Catalyst

  1. Weigh & press. Load ~300 mg of catalyst into the sample-prep pressing die and press into a pellet on the Carver hydraulic press — do not exceed 4 metric tons.
  2. Remove the pellet. Remove the piston rod and anvils. If the anvils are stuck, place two spare anvils on the bottom and gently press to lift them free.
  3. Clean the die. Wipe with Kimwipes → blow with house air → wipe with an acetone-soaked Kimwipe → blow dry. Ensure no sample remains on the inner wall.
  4. Crush & sieve. Gently crush the pellet with a mortar and pestle (do not grind to a fine powder). Sieve at 212 µm. Repeat until you have enough material.
Quartz wool safetyLoad quartz wool in the fume hood — never in open air. It can irritate the lungs. Keep the container closed when not in use.
02

Load the Reactor

  1. Mark the thermocouple position. Use a stainless-steel rod to measure and mark the length of the thermocouple on the reactor setup before loading, so the catalyst ends up at the correct depth.
  2. Load catalyst. Using the marked rod as a guide, load the sieved catalyst into the ¼" stainless-steel reactor, sandwiched between two small pinches of quartz wool.
  3. Connect the reactor. Connect the ¼" reactor to the gas line and furnace. Insert the thermocouple in the same direction as the stainless-steel rod until it touches the quartz wool.
03

Reduction & Pressurization

  1. Switch to reactor flow. Switch the 3-way valve to the reactor and start flowing pure H₂ at 40 ml/min (or the recommended rate).
  2. Pressurize to 50 psi. Turn the pressure regulator to 50 psi (or recommended). Check for leaks with Snoop at all inlet/outlet connections — bubbles = leak; tighten.
  3. Heat the outlet line. Start the heating tape on the reactor outlet / GC inlet. Set the Variable Autotransformer to ~40% (~125 °C) to prevent water condensation in the GC line.
  4. Start the reduction program. Run the reduction heating program — typically ramp at 10 °C/min to 350 °C, hold 2 hours.

04

Running the GC Sequence (Bypass First)

  1. Set reaction gas flowrates. In the Brooks Smart software, enter the specific reaction gas flowrates (see Section 06). Reduce pressure to 0 psi to flush out the bypass, then pressurize to 300 psi.
  2. Start the GC sequence. Start the sequence as: 4–5 bypass runs → 1 hr spacer → 36 data points (12 hr run) → standby. Confirm the correct acquisition method is selected.
  3. Monitor the bypass TCD. Confirm only reactant gases appear in the TCD at the expected retention times (below). Nitrogen appearing could indicate air entry.
    GasExpected TCD retention time
    H₂~2.75 min
    CO₂~3.5 min
    Ar~6 min
    CO~10.5 min
If nitrogen appears in the TCDSet the GC back-pressure regulator to ~5 psi. If it was just residual air in the line, it will disappear after the first 1–2 bypass runs.
05

Switching Bypass ↔ Reactor

Once the bypass runs look clean, switch the flow from bypass to the reactor:

  1. Close the bypass outlet.
  2. Switch the three-way valve to the reactor.
  3. Open the reactor outlet.
  4. Wait a few minutes; confirm the GC is at 300 psi.
  5. Double-check gas flowrates, valve positions, temperature, and heating tapes.
GC method filesUse 242_PlotQ_90C.amx (detects alcohols, ~20 min run) or 242_GasPro (heavier hydrocarbons, ~25 min run). Check with lab members if a file name has changed.

07

Pre-Run Safety Checklist

Before starting any experiment, run through this checklist:

  • Catalyst loaded and reactor connected properly
  • All connections checked with Snoop — no leaks
  • Gas cylinder pressures all > 300 psi
  • Heating tape wrapped correctly (not on itself)
  • Variable Autotransformer set to ~40% for the outlet line
  • Correct GC acquisition method selected
  • FID and TCD temperatures stable
  • Brooks software showing correct flowrates
  • GC back-pressure regulator set to ~5 psi
  • Quartz wool loaded in the fume hood

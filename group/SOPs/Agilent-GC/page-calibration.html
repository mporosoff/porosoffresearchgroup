Section A — Agilent 7890 GC (U2136)

GC Calibration

Multi-point calibration of reactant gases, validation, and single-point calibration of hydrocarbon products with the refinery standard.

Tag: U2136 Model: Agilent 7890 GC S/N: US17383018 Location: G242 Updated: July 2026
On this page

  • Validation

01

Calibration for Reactant Gases (Brooks MFCs)

  1. Within the Agilent Method Development software, open the processing method and in the Compounds subsection, select Calibration to see the screen shown below.
  2. Identify compounds to be recalibrated by noting which levels contain non-zero values.
  3. Save the processing method as a new file name.
  4. Delete values in all unwanted level channels associated with the compound to be recalibrated. (See the example below for CO calibration, where Level 20 [green] is zeroed.)
  5. Determine the usual operating concentration of the reactant/product and build calibration points clustered around the concentrations where you actually operate.

    Example: carbon monoxide is a reactant in FTS and a product in RWGS, so usual concentrations cluster near 100% and 0% — reflected in the level table below, with Ar as the diluent.

    Gas composition (vol%)L21L22L23L24L25L26L27L28L29L30
    Carbon monoxide351030407075809095
    Argon9795907060302520105
  6. Flow the desired concentration of the compound (often diluted in Ar inert) and, using the 242 back-pressure regulator, pressurize the system to reaction pressure (usually 300 psi). Allow to equilibrate for 15 minutes.
  7. Set up a Single Sample injection within the Data Acquisition software with the correct 242 Acquisition method (refer to the 242 SOPs for operation), checking the box to store injection values as “Calibration” at a designated level column.
  8. Enter the current concentration of the desired compound and diluent (if used) in the designated level column.
  9. Perform the single sample injection 3 times for each concentration/level so values can be averaged.
  10. Repeat for the other concentrations of the compound, adjusting the amount of diluent used.
Curves requiredCalibration curves of 4–5 points are necessary for: methane, CO, CO₂, Ar, H₂, and any new reactant gas.
02

Validating the Calibration

  1. Once calibration is complete, flow the gas at a known concentration that falls within the cluster of points on the calibration curve, without selecting the Calibration box, and compare the reported Amount to the known concentration.
  2. If the result is within 0.5% of the known concentration, the calibration is complete.
  3. If it is beyond 0.5%, repeat the calibration procedure above.
03

Single-Point Calibration for Hydrocarbon Products

Single-point calibration is effective for hydrocarbon products of RWGS or FTS, using the refinery calibration gas standard. If a new reactant is used, run the full multi-point calibration in Section 01 instead.

  1. Repeat steps 1–4 of the reactant-gas calibration above (open Calibration screen, save method under a new name, clear unwanted levels).
  2. Using the calibration gas standard in the cylinder shown below, enter all component concentrations in one single level row, ensuring the components sum to 100%.

    Example: 20% methane, 10% ethane, 60% hydrogen, 5% ethylene, 5% propylene all listed on one previously-cleared calibration level.

  3. Connect the refinery calibration gas to the 245 reactor inlet fittings (pictured below).
  4. Ensuring the shutoff valve is closed, open the calibration gas cylinder and adjust the pressure to 15 psi by pulling down on the black plastic cylinder below the gauge and rotating.
  5. Set up a Single Sample injection within the Data Acquisition software with the correct 245 Acquisition method (refer to the 245 SOPs), checking the box to store injection values as “Calibration” at a designated level column.
  6. Perform the injection by opening the shutoff valve ~5 seconds before clicking Run, and leave the calibration gas open for ~30 seconds after the injection before closing the shutoff valve.
  7. Repeat 3 times at the same calibration level to average the calibration points.
